# Module 1: Agile Principles & Frameworks

## Research & Learn

### 1. What is Agile, and how does it differ from traditional project management?

Agile is a way of working where teams break large tasks into smaller pieces, complete them step by step, and make improvements based on feedback. Instead of planning everything at the beginning, Agile allows teams to adapt when requirements or priorities change. Traditional project management usually follows a more structured plan, with specific stages and deadlines decided early in the project. Agile is more flexible and encourages regular communication, teamwork, and continuous improvement.

### 2. What are the core values and principles of Agile?

The Agile Manifesto has four core values:

- **Individuals and interactions over processes and tools:** People communicating and working together is more important than simply following processes.
- **Working software over comprehensive documentation:** Delivering something that works is more valuable than spending too much time on documentation.
- **Customer collaboration over contract negotiation:** Working closely with customers helps teams understand their needs and deliver better results.
- **Responding to change over following a plan:** Teams should be willing to adjust their plans when they discover a better approach.

Agile also has 12 principles that focus on customer satisfaction, delivering work regularly, teamwork, adapting to change, maintaining quality, and continuously improving how the team works.

### 3. What are the differences between Scrum and Kanban, and when is each used?

**Scrum** divides work into fixed periods called sprints, usually lasting one to four weeks. The team plans its work at the beginning of each sprint, reviews the results, and discusses what could be improved. It is useful when a team benefits from structured planning and regular delivery cycles.

**Kanban** focuses on visualising work on a board and moving tasks through different stages, such as To Do, In Progress, and Done. It does not require fixed sprints, so team members can prioritise and complete tasks as needs change. Kanban is useful when work arrives continuously and priorities may change frequently.

### 4. Why does Focus Bear lean towards Kanban instead of Scrum?

I think Kanban suits Focus Bear because it allows the team to work flexibly and adjust priorities when needed. In a product-focused environment, new ideas, feedback, and issues can come up at any time. With Kanban, the team can see what everyone is working on, identify tasks that are taking too long, and focus on completing existing work before starting too many new tasks. This can improve transparency, reduce unnecessary pressure, and help the team maintain a healthy balance between productivity and well-being.

## Reflection

### 1. What do you think are the biggest benefits and challenges of Agile?

I think one of the biggest benefits of Agile is flexibility. It allows teams to respond to feedback, solve problems earlier, and make improvements without having to follow a fixed plan that may no longer suit the project. Another benefit is better communication because team members regularly share updates, discuss challenges, and work towards common goals. However, Agile can also be challenging. Priorities may change frequently, and without clear communication, team members might become confused about what they should focus on. It can also be difficult to estimate how long tasks will take. I think Agile works best when everyone understands their responsibilities, communicates openly, and stays focused on the team's priorities.

### 2. How can Agile principles help improve your role and responsibilities?

As a QA professional, I believe Agile principles can help me work more closely with developers and other team members throughout the development process. Instead of waiting until the end to test a feature, I can get involved earlier, understand the requirements, identify potential risks, and provide feedback as the work progresses. Agile also encourages continuous improvement, which is important in QA because there is always an opportunity to improve test coverage, automation, and defect reporting. By communicating issues early and adapting to changing requirements, I can help the team deliver a more reliable product and provide a better experience for users.

### 1. Summarize the main differences between Scrum and Kanban in your own words.

Scrum and Kanban both help teams organise work and deliver better results, but they manage work differently. 

Scrum uses fixed-length sprints, with the team deciding what to complete during each sprint. It has defined roles and regular meetings to help the team plan, review progress, and improve.

Kanban is more flexible because work moves continuously through a visual board without requiring fixed sprints. It helps the team manage its workload, identify bottlenecks, and focus on finishing tasks before taking on too much new work.

Personally, I think Kanban is a good choice for teams like Focus Bear because it supports changing priorities and makes it easier to see what needs attention. Scrum can be more suitable when a team benefits from structured planning and working towards specific sprint goals.
